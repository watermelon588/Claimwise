# Evidence register

What we know about how health insurance claims go wrong for patients and their families in India, where that knowledge comes from and how strong it is. This register is the research base for every later design decision.

Scope: India, because every source found is Indian (assumption AS-01 in [decisions](../decisions.md)). Sources are listed with links in [sources](sources.md) and cited here by ID.

## How to read this register

Every statement carries one of four labels. They are kept apart on purpose.

| Label | Meaning | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Fact | Stated in a regulation, official statistic or published order. Says what should happen or what was counted, not what claimants experience. | Section 2 |
| Self-reported | One claimant's firsthand public account. Real, but unverified and not representative on its own. | Section 1 |
| Pattern | The same thing reported in two or more independent accounts. Stronger than one account, still not a measured rate. | Section 3 |
| Hypothesis | Our claim to test. Never presented as a finding. | Section 7 |

IDs: E = situation entry, RF = regulatory or statistical fact, PT = pattern, X = contradiction, H = hypothesis, AS = assumption.

## 2. Regulatory and statistical facts

What the rules say should happen. Page numbers refer to the PDF pages of the source.

| ID | Fact | Source | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| RF-01 | Cashless authorization decided within 1 hour of the request; final discharge authorization within 3 hours. Extra hospital charges caused by a delay beyond 3 hours are borne by the insurer. | S-03 p9; S-04 p30-31 | Clear timers the agent can track and flag. |
| RF-02 | Claims other than cashless settled within 15 days of the claim being submitted. | S-04 p31, p70 | The reimbursement deadline claimants do not know (see X-03). |
| RF-03 | If not settled in time, interest at bank rate plus 2 percent from intimation to payment, paid without being asked. | S-04 p30 | A deterministic calculation, never a model estimate. |
| RF-04 | After intimation, the insurer or TPA collects the required documents from the hospital. The policyholder is not required to submit them. | S-03 p9; S-04 p31 | Shapes how a document request should be answered (see X-01). |
| RF-05 | No claim shall be rejected or closed for want of documents. | S-04 p30, p58 | Grounds to challenge a "closed" status. |
| RF-06 | No repudiation without approval of a Claims Review Committee; every rejection or partial disallowance must give full reasons citing specific policy terms. | S-03 p9, p12; S-04 p31 | A decision letter can be checked for a missing clause. |
| RF-07 | With several indemnity policies, the claimant chooses the primary insurer; if its cover falls short, that insurer coordinates the balance with the others. Benefit policies are claimed from each insurer. | S-03 p10; S-04 p32-33 | The household with two covers; the choice belongs to the claimant. |
| RF-08 | Group policy claims are paid to the employee or beneficiary; no claim denied for missing member details. | S-03 p13; S-04 p32 | Employer cover as a second policy. |
| RF-09 | Grievances acknowledged immediately and resolved within 14 days with reasons. Complaints can be filed and tracked on Bima Bharosa. | S-04 p33-34, p70-71 | First escalation step and its timer. |
| RF-10 | If the grievance answer is unsatisfactory, or after 30 days, the claimant may go to the Insurance Ombudsman for claims up to Rs 50 lakh, free of charge, online or in writing. | S-04 p34, p67, p71 | Eligibility has to be checked, never promised. |
| RF-11 | Insurers comply with an Ombudsman award within 30 days, with penal interest, and a per-day penalty if not honoured unless appealed. | S-03 p10; S-04 p34, p60 | Follow-through after a win (E-09). |
| RF-12 | Express consent of the policyholder is needed for every instance of sharing medical records in the ABHA context. A person with disability may authorize a representative. | S-03 p17; S-04 p24 | Consent is per share. General caregiver authority is not defined in these texts. |
| RF-13 | Moratorium after 60 months of continuous cover: no contest on non-disclosure except established fraud. Renewal cannot be denied for past claims. | S-03 p7-8; S-04 p29-30, p58-59 | Continuing-cover questions after a case closes. |
| RF-14 | When the problem is the bank (insurer paid, credit wrong or missing), the RBI scheme applies: complain to the bank first, then the RBI Ombudsman after 30 days without a reply or after an unsatisfactory one. | S-05 | A separate route from insurance grievances. |
| RF-15 | The Insurance Ombudsman network received 52,575 complaints in FY 2023-24 across all insurance lines. In health, 95 percent of the entertainable complaints disposed of were about partial or total repudiation of claims. | S-24 p7, p87 | Repudiation disputes are the main health complaint type, beyond individual anecdotes. Per-insurer health figures are pending a visual check of the report's tables and are not used yet. |
| RF-16 | A District Consumer Commission held a hospital deficient for billing the full amount at discharge without disclosing the lower TPA-approved amount; the claimant found it only by asking the TPA. | S-26 | The patient can be the last to learn the approved amount. |

## 3. Repeated patterns

| ID | Pattern | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| PT-01 | The same or renamed document is requested again after it was sent, and each request restarts with a new handler. | E-06, E-07, E-12; replies in C-06 describe 7 uploads and a similar case at another insurer | Across 2 insurers, 3 accounts plus replies |
| PT-02 | Claimants cannot prove what they sent, when and to whom, so they re-send instead of contesting. | E-06, E-07, E-10 | 3 accounts |
| PT-03 | Decisions arrive without the clause-specific reasons the rules require, and reasons change between channels. | E-08, E-09, E-07 | 3 accounts |
| PT-04 | Progress often starts only after an outside escalation (Bima Bharosa, Ombudsman, IRDAI), and the first win may still need follow-up. | E-05, E-06, E-09; E-07 shows escalation can also stall | 4 accounts |
| PT-05 | Family members manage claims for parents or children with no visible formal authority. | E-01, E-04, E-06, E-07 | 4 accounts; S-27 says insurers refuse unauthorized relatives |
| PT-06 | Households with two covers do not know which to use first or how the balance moves between them. | E-02, E-03, E-04 | 3 accounts, all asking strangers |
| PT-07 | One medical event becomes several claims (interim and final cashless, then reimbursement; main claim then pre and post hospitalization) with separate deadlines. | E-01, E-04, E-11 | 3 accounts |
| PT-08 | "Approved" or "settled" is not the end: amounts are reduced, deposits stay held, or money does not arrive. | E-01, E-10, E-13, RF-16 | 4 sources |

## 4. The six research angles

| Angle | What the evidence shows | Entries | Still open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preparation and authority | Families act for patients without formal authority; insurers recognise a registered authorized representative, a power of attorney or the policyholder's own login, and nominees have no rights while the policyholder is alive. | PT-05, RF-12, S-27 | Authority rules per insurer; how consent changes during a case |
| Route and coordination | Cashless and reimbursement differ in who submits and what deadline applies; one event often uses both; the hospital desk ends at discharge. | E-01, E-06, E-10, RF-01, RF-02, PT-07 | Settlement deadline from insurer to hospital for cashless claims |
| Evidence and requests | Rules say the insurer collects documents; practice shows repeated and renamed requests and unaccepted proof of submission. | PT-01, PT-02, RF-04, RF-05, X-01 | Whether insurers record document receipt anywhere the claimant can see |
| Decision and disagreement | Partial approvals, reversals and unexplained rejections; some disputes need a clinician (medical necessity) or a lawyer. | E-08, E-09, PT-03, RF-06 | How often reasons are missing |
| Escalation and resolution | A defined ladder (insurer grievance, Bima Bharosa, Ombudsman, consumer forum) with timers that claimants confuse. | RF-09 to RF-11, PT-04, X-02 | Who may file on another's behalf on Bima Bharosa |
| Payment and continuing cover | "Settled" without credit; held deposits; approved amount hidden at discharge; a reinstated policy needed its own follow-up. | PT-08, E-09, E-13, RF-13, RF-14 | A case where the bank credit differs from the settlement letter |

## 5. Contradictory evidence

The research does not only show failure.

- The process sometimes works: E-01 ended with a smooth reimbursement, and replies in C-01 and C-09 report claims settled without trouble or after a single filing. Many claims never reach a forum, so the cases above are skewed toward problems.
- Community advice in E-02 and E-03 was accurate and quick. For some questions a forum already answers well.
- In E-01 the hospital desk solved the immediate confusion.

Tensions between sources:

| ID | Tension | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| X-01 | Rules say the policyholder need not submit documents, yet insurer checklists are called indicative and claimants are asked for the same documents repeatedly. | RF-04; S-22; PT-01 |
| X-02 | Grievance resolution is 14 days in the circular and Bima Bharosa homepage text, 15 days in the Bima Bharosa FAQ. | RF-09; S-06 |
| X-03 | Claimants cite 30 days, a TPA cites 90 days, the circular says 15 days for claims other than cashless. No final-settlement deadline for cashless claims was found in the reviewed texts. | E-10; RF-02 |
| X-04 | Clause-specific reasons are required, yet decisions arrive as "NOT JUSTIFIED", "not admissible" or a phone call. | RF-06; PT-03 |
| X-05 | "No claim shall be closed for want of documents", yet claims are reported as rejected for documents "not provided by the customer". | RF-05; E-07 |

## 6. Access limits

What the product could and could not connect to, as far as public evidence shows.

| System | What was found | Access label |
|---|---|---|
| Insurer and TPA claim systems | Portals, apps, email and phone. No documented public API for claimants. | Manual handoff |
| NHCX (national health claims exchange) | FHIR-based message flows for payers and providers covering eligibility, pre-authorization, claims and payment notices. Beneficiaries are not shown as message participants. | Partner-only |
| Bima Bharosa | Web portal with complaint token and status tracking. No API described. | Manual handoff |
| Insurance Ombudsman | Online complaint form and hearings. | Manual handoff |
| RBI complaint system | Online portal for bank-side complaints. | Manual handoff |
| Email notifications from insurers | Status emails exist (S-08); reading them with the user's permission is plausible. | Unverified |

The absence of a claimant-side API comes from a limited search. It is a finding about what is documented, not proof that nothing exists.

Research access limits: Reddit blocks automated reading, so threads were read from copies saved manually; forum accounts are skewed toward problems; no interviews have been run; no insurer, TPA or hospital staff view is included yet.

| ID | Hypothesis | Evidence so far | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| H-01 | Repeated and renamed requests are a cross-insurer pattern. | PT-01 | Supported, self-reported |
| H-02 | A shared, dated record of what was sent would let claimants contest repeats instead of re-sending. | PT-02, RF-05 | Untested |
| H-03 | Partial settlements are hard to reconcile because deductions, co-pay and bank credit arrive in different documents and channels. | E-01, E-08, E-13, RF-16 | Partially supported; no mismatch case yet |
| H-04 | Families acting for patients need authority captured up front, before a claim starts. | PT-05, S-27 | Supported for practice; insurer rules unverified |

| H-06 | Late or missing escalation is a major failure point. | PT-04, X-02, X-03 | Supported in 3 accounts |

## 8. Reasons the agent may not be worth building

1. **Cashless is growing and the rules move document collection to the insurer** (RF-04; the circular asks insurers to aim for 100 percent cashless, S-03 p9). If that works, preparation pain shrinks. Counter-evidence: cashless was denied or partial in E-01, E-06 and E-10, and the hospital deposit in E-10 shows follow-through work remains.
2. **Human claim help already exists** (see [alternatives](alternatives.md)): brokers, advisors and the hospital desk do some of this for free. Value would have to come from continuity across the whole case, not from one step.
3. **No system access**: with every integration a manual handoff, the agent can prepare, track and remind but cannot submit or confirm on its own. If users must still do every action themselves, the gain may be too small.
4. **The hard decisions are not administrative**: medical necessity (E-08) and legal disputes need specialists, and the agent must not pretend otherwise.

These are kept open. The brief and lifecycle documents must show how each is answered, or narrow the scope.

## 9. Gaps

1. **Bank credit differs from settlement letter (branch challenge 2).** On 2026-11-07, a targeted public search found short-settlement disputes and accounts of a settled status without a credit. The reviewed candidates did not establish both the settlement-letter amount and a different received bank credit for the same payment. E-13 distinguishes portal status from bank credit. The Ombudsman award S-30 records a smaller credit than the claimed amount and a request for a deduction breakdown. It does not establish a letter-to-credit difference. The exact mismatch remains a synthetic design scenario.
2. **Caregiver consent changes mid-claim (branch challenge 3).** On 2026-11-07, a targeted public search found representative and consent material and accounts of relatives managing claims. The National Health Authority policy S-31 describes withdrawable health-data consent. The reviewed candidates did not document an actual withdrawal or change of representative authority during an active claim with another relevant cover. This remains a design hypothesis.
3. Alternatives not yet checked at source: broker claim services, advisor support, an insurer app's tracking.
4. Official IRDAI page links for S-03 and S-04; later amendments to the 2024 circulars.
5. No interviews or observed workflows yet.

## What is not claimed

No market size, adoption rate or willingness to pay is claimed anywhere in this register. The Ombudsman figures (RF-15) describe complaint volume, not demand for a product.
